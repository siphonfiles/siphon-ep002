# Ep. 002 subtitles — translation rules

Source: `source.json` = the 197 English caption cues of the documentary (YouTube channel Siphon Files, episode 2, about the Bitget exchange hack of 24 September 2026). Each cue has `id`, `sec` (how long it is on screen) and `en` (the text). The English is final and verified; the translation must say exactly the same thing, nothing more, nothing less.

**What the episode is:** a calm, factual investigative documentary narrated by Thomas Archer, an AI narrator. About $388M left Bitget's hot and warm wallets in under three hours. Bitget says no private key was stolen: attackers came in through a zero-day in third-party *security* products ("Product A" and "Product B", vendor unnamed), stole a real employee's credentials, reached the wallet job server, forged the answers of the risk-control checks, and Bitget's own signing service signed the withdrawals. At 19:05 UTC the alarm rang and Bitget blocked all *customer* withdrawals, but the attacker's forged requests kept being signed for another 2 h 39 min. Analysts (Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs) lean towards North Korea; no government has attributed it. In February 2025 Bitget had lent the robbed exchange Bybit 40,000 ether.

## Output
Write `<code>.json` in this folder: a JSON array with one object per cue, in the same order, `{"id": <same id>, "text": "<translation>"}`. Exactly 197 objects, every id from source.json, none empty. One string per cue, **no line breaks** (the build script wraps lines). UTF-8, no escaping of non-ASCII characters.

## Hard rules
1. **Meaning first, exactly.** Do not add, drop, soften or strengthen anything. Keep every hedge and attribution exactly: "Bitget says", "according to one analysis", "Mandiant dates…", "ZachXBT says", "lean the same way", "may have been", "investigators found", "the reports don't say", "for now". Nobody is accused beyond what the English says. "Suspected" stays suspected.
2. **Cue boundaries are sacred.** Each cue is translated on its own line; when an English sentence runs over two cues (cues whose English does not end in . ? or !), split the translation at the same point in a way that reads naturally in your language, even if word order forces you to move a few words between the two halves. Never move content more than one cue.
3. **Length.** Subtitles must be readable at the speed shown in `sec`. Aim for a translation no longer than ~1.25× the English character count (CJK: ~0.6×). Prefer concise, natural spoken phrasing over literal wording. No filler.
4. **Names never translated or transliterated** (keep Latin spelling, in every language including ja/ko/zh/ar/hi/ru/uk): Bitget, Bybit, Binance, Mandiant, SlowMist, Halborn, DFNS, Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs, ZachXBT, THORChain, Zcash, CoinJoin, Kelp DAO, TraderTraitor, Gracy Chen, Thomas Archer, Siphon Files, FBI, CEO (may be the local word for CEO), Product A, Product B (or "produsul A"-style local word for "product" + A/B, same in all cues), VPN, IP, USDT, TRX, XRP, ether / ETH, Bitcoin, Ethereum, UTC.
5. **Numbers, times, dates: the same values.** Keep times as 19:05, 18:31, 21:23 etc. Use your language's normal decimal/thousand separators (40.000 / 40 000 / 0,84 are fine) and normal date order ("24 września", "9月24日"). Amounts: $388 million → the natural local form ("388 millones de dólares", "3億8800万ドル", "3.88 億美元", "38.8 करोड़ डॉलर" are all fine) but the value must be identical. Never round or convert. "about" stays "about".
6. **Terminology, consistent across the whole file:** hot wallet / warm wallet / cold wallet (use the term your crypto community uses; keep English in parentheses only if there is no local term, at most once), signing service, risk control, reconciliation, zero-day, web shell, appliance, wallet job server, malicious packages, gas (Ethereum fee limit), stablecoins, mixing (CoinJoin), private/shielded pool, launderers, permissionless, protection fund, withdrawal.
7. **Register:** calm documentary narration, second person "you" = the viewer (choose the natural form: tú/você/vous/Sie/sen/tu/あなた (or omit), 你, etc., and keep it consistent). Short sentences stay short; fragments stay fragments ("No interest. No collateral.").
8. **Written, not spoken, conventions** of your language: correct punctuation, quotation marks, capitalisation, spacing (French non-breaking space before ? ! : ; is fine as a normal space), no English punctuation habits where they are wrong locally.
9. No notes, brackets or translator comments.
